# SEIRViz-derived style profile

This profile was distilled from the SEIRViz manuscript PDFs in `OldsplitPaper/` and is intended for safe style transfer only.

## 1. Voice and tone

- Formal academic prose with thesis/manuscript cadence
- Explanatory rather than minimalist
- Frequent emphasis on significance, appropriateness, and practical relevance
- Consistently frames methods in terms of clinical utility, interpretability, and decision support

## 2. Preferred paragraph structure

1. Name the topic or phase immediately.
2. Explain what the study/system does.
3. Justify why the method or setting is appropriate.
4. Close by linking the choice to objectives, applicability, or expected benefit.

## 3. Favored rhetorical moves

- Define the concept before elaborating on procedure
- Introduce the phase, then narrate its purpose
- Explain inclusion criteria and data sources in complete prose before details
- Revisit the study objective at the end of sections
- Translate technical steps into practical implications for clinicians or users

## 4. Sentence patterns

- Medium-to-long sentences joined by causal connectors
- Frequent use of transition adverbs at the start of sentences
- Uses explicit phrasing like `This study employs`, `This phase focuses on`, `The dataset will include`, `The selected healthcare facilities will provide`
- Often pairs a method statement with a rationale in the same sentence

## 5. Word-choice tendencies

- Repeats formal academic verbs: `employs`, `enables`, `supports`, `ensures`, `integrates`, `identifies`, `evaluates`
- Prefers domain-explicit nouns: `clinical applicability`, `structured records`, `predictive model`, `feature selection`, `risk stratification`, `decision-making`
- Uses intensifiers sparingly but consistently: `significantly`, `systematically`, `critically`, `particularly`

## 6. Heading and formatting patterns

- Strong preference for labeled major sections and named subsections
- Methodology often broken into phases with descriptive labels
- Figures are embedded as part of the explanatory flow
- Lists are used for steps, criteria, or model procedures, but most exposition stays in paragraph form

## 7. Figure and table introduction patterns

- Visuals are framed as methodological aids, not decorative inserts
- The surrounding prose explains the operational purpose of the figure/table
- Tables typically summarize included variables, decisions, or evaluation criteria

## 8. Citation behavior

- Citations support broad factual claims, epidemiology, medical rationale, or methodological precedent
- Claims are typically stated first, then cited parenthetically
- Citation density is moderate: enough to signal scholarship, not so dense that every line is burdened

## 9. Safe imitation checklist

When rewriting:

- Keep the source chapter's facts and structure
- Upgrade topic sentences so each paragraph clearly announces its purpose
- Add transitions only when they improve flow
- Tie procedures back to study goals or utility
- Prefer full prose explanations for rationale
- Keep distinctive phrasing original

## 10. What to avoid

- Do not clone exact transition chains from the paper repeatedly
- Do not copy distinctive phrases such as highly specific explanatory clauses
- Do not imitate any factual claims that belong only to the SEIRViz study
- Do not inflate simple statements into overly repetitive prose
- Do not add fake citations just to match the tone
