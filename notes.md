Justification of Model Threshold and Asymmetric Misclassification Costs
"In evaluating the clinical utility of the DIANA screening model, we adopted an asymmetric cost-sensitive approach to misclassification. As established in epidemiological screening frameworks (Maxim et al., 2014), preventative screening tools must inherently prioritize Sensitivity (Recall) to minimize False Negatives. In the context of metabolic dysfunction, the clinical and economic cost of a False Negative—discharging an at-risk patient without warning, leading to delayed intervention and irreversible microvascular complications—far outweighs the cost of a False Positive. A False Positive generated by our non-invasive algorithm simply results in a recommendation for a routine, low-cost diagnostic blood test (e.g., HbA1c or FBS). Recent economic evaluations of medical artificial intelligence further support this architecture; Xie et al. (2024) demonstrated that optimizing AI screening models for high sensitivity over specificity yields the most cost-effective outcomes in diabetic populations. Therefore, the DIANA model’s decision threshold was intentionally calibrated to maximize sensitivity (75%), ensuring it safely captures the maximum number of true at-risk trajectories before requiring laboratory confirmation."
